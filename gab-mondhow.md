> Since you hold the position that content warnings are arbitrary, and because of that anything could be made a content warning, therefore you don't have a moral obligation to use them, would you say that, e.g. sending unsolicited gore to people in private message channels has the same moral value as sending them random gibberish, e.g. it's morally neutral, since voluntary looking at either gore or random gibberish would be okay? So never mind anyone that could get triggered or traumatized by it, since they implicitly agree to being shown arbitrary things on the Internet, so in a sense it would be like clicking a goatse link, the only difference being that there is a considerable delay between the action (e.g. joining a Discord guild with DMs allowed from non-friends in that guild) and the result (the unsolicited gore)?

More specifically, my position is that trauma is complex enough that people can be triggered by anything-ever, and at regularly unpredictable rates. As a result, we don't 
have a collective obligation to rearrange our communications infrastructure around the possibility of being triggered. A person with an eating disorder could be triggered by 
seeing a person eat an ice cream cone while walking down the street. When it comes to localized social spaces, or dynamics that obtain between ongoing friendships, then there 
are either mild obligations or elective praiseworthy acts that can be described in this area. E.g. if you know a friend is an arachnophobe, then sending them pictures of 
ghastly spiders is inconsiderate, and thus carries some microcosmic moral weight, even if it's not a dramatic and major wrong. When it comes to showing random people gore 
pictures, it's probably morally neutral as a stochastic act made in the absence of particular information. (E.g. sending it to a stranger is different from sending it to a 
person whom you know with >50% probability is likely to have a panic attack.) It never arises to the level of a severe wrong in any particular case, however. There is 
praiseworthiness and virtue to be found in keeping a clean information ecosystem where people are not subject to aesthetically grotesque images unnecessarily and involuntary 
— and this has downstream social benefits — but never is there a strong moral obligation to act in this way.

> Why is it okay for an aromantic non-asexual to "lead people on" for sex? Is that not manipulation, by definition? Standpoint epistemology check: I am aromantic and not
> asexual.

All relationships are organic experiments with no pre-determined end. They can be short-lasting, they can be long-lasting, and most of them are doomed to failure. 'Leading a 
person on' is within the natural flow of flirting and mutual discovery: it is not too different from, say, displaying more interest in a person than you actually have for the 
sake of continuing and exploring the relationship. Even deciding that one truly loves another is, at least in mature cases, a long-term beat point, and cannot be decided 
within a short period of time. It's not as if the aromantic person is masking an absence at every stage of the process: plenty of romantic people fall into relationships in 
which there are no definitive feelings they'd consider true and non-ephemeral love. As a result, an aromantic and sexual person can participate in a series of especially 
short-term relationships without there being a functional difference. There is always the quasi-manipulative promise 'of more,' which may or may not come to pass, when people 
are discovering each other in an intimate way. You do not have an obligation to realize that 'something more' at some future time, whether one is romantic or aromantic, as it 
can only be met by involuntary circumstance. The aromantic person is barely exceptional in this regard.

> I think non-Humean notions of causation easily lead to dubious notions, such as assuming that causation is some universal fact,
> and thus to determinism and imputing that
> some mysterious substance, whether Kantian noumena or physical substance, underlies conscious experience while remaining
> inaccessible to consciousness.

This notion, ironically, makes the most sense if we interpret things in terms of metatheoretically Humean notions. Which has been 
a genuine systemic bias toward libertarian free-will, only being recently revealed and unravelled on a large scale. If we, for 
example, take causation to be primarily found in substances manifesting their causal powers, where agents like human-beings are 
well-ordered substances that reflect on their capacities in reasons-responsive ways, then the agential will itself, constituted 
in part from their physical powers and in part from their deliberative capacities to contemplate decisions, is a ubiquitous and 
non-surprising aspect of this world. It is not too different from how other substances manifest their causal powers, it's just 
that we have the introspective and deliberative capacities to manifest our powers in alignment with our intentions. Causation 
having a 'universal' characteristic leads to neither determinism nor a lack of free-will.

It should be noted that substances here are just bearers of properties. There is neither “mystery” nor something uniquely 
inaccessible to consciousness present here. Although it should be noted that at least some things being inaccessible to 
consciousness neither implies determinism nor a lack of free-will: e.g. I don't need to know the natural facts behind my 
autonomic nervous system in order to utilize its capacities toward my intentions. Factors outside your conscious awareness limit 
your abilities - they can be control-undermining conditions - but they do not wholesale undermine the autonomy of your decisions. 
For example, I can't choose to run if I have my legs cut off, but this is a novel physical limitation on my potential power: not 
a destruction of my agent-causal ability to do otherwise (ala the principle of alternate possibilities.) We always make 
decisions with the expectation of some kind of force or constraint that hinders us. By the same analysis, I don't need to know 
everything about how metaphysical substance works in order to be a substance with agential freedom.
