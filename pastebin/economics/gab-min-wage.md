#### Gabriel Amadej:
what do you think of the minimum wage? i remember there was a series of articles where carson and johnson went toe-to-toe on the issue. carson's position was that it was a (mostly) beneficial second-order constraint on the effects of the state-capitalist economy, and johnson's position being that it makes things even worse -- particularly with how workplace management reacts to it. i've been reaching out into other literature on the issue but i still can't make-up my mind.

#### Jason Lee Byas:
I tend to take the fairly standard libertarian view of minimum wage. Admittedly, I haven't read anything from Kevin on it, but it seems to 1.) be pretty straightforwardly aggressive, even in the larger context, 2.) have clearly bad economic consequences, and 3.) benefit larger companies at the expense of smaller ones.

But if you have points that might make me think otherwise (or something to link on it), I'd be interested.

#### Gabriel Amadej:
Caveat: Some of what I wrote here might seem aggressive but I'm really tired and irritable at the present moment. Please forgive that! I also did not say as much as I wanted to say, but I shall do that later. I'm really lazy and inconsistent in these things. I'm attempting to start a dialogue I can learn, and not so much a debate.

-I agree that 3.) is true. And I find it to be the most compelling argument against the minimum wage to be honest.

-For 2.) -- as far as i have perused with the literature -- there isn't very good evidence to affirm either the positive or negative claim on this issue. At least for the typical 'bad economic consequences' that are cited. The standard libertarian view on this issue is -- to be a tad hostile -- just garbage. At least as far as my exposure to it goes. The marginal utility argument is amongst the worst. Second only to 'it will cause unemployment even amongst the adult labour force': A claim for which there is absolutely zero evidence for; 'evidence' both in the sense of evidentialist a priori reasons and general empirical analysis. There is more arguments than that, of course, but I've no wish to continue on and put words in your mouth.

I am, however, open to there being serious negative economic consequences. For example:  A. Reactionary managerial actions such as tighter shifts, pace increases, and other arbitrary, irrational panics of management; B. What you said in 3.); C. Teens being effectively choked out of the labour force; D. A facade that does not keep up with labour productivity nor inflation, which just gives short-term gains for workers at one point in time -- and keeps them more docile -- while the bosses gain in the long term because overtime the wages they pay them is effectively less. This is especially worsened by the fact that all effective labour activist tactics are illegal and suppressed. As well as that, undeniably, the gains in labour productivity are enclosed as artificial sources of profit. So, in essence, the minimum wage is a wool placed under the eyes of unaware labourers, progressive liberals, and blowhard idiots that think the 1930s+ was a victory for labour. (/Groan)

I am curious, however, what exactly you have in mind when you cite the 'standard libertarian view here'. For the examples I cited were just an arbitrary selection and I've no wish to box the position into something it is not.

-For 1.) How is it aggressive even vis-a-vis the 'larger context'? I am curious about your thinking here. Unlike a freed market, wages and work conditions are determined by the institutional structures that are causally created and maintained under the forceful state-capitalist nexus. In my view, the powers that be enforce -- violently when needed -- an artificial property right on the product and surplus value created by the wage-labourer. I see the statocracy and plutocracy as constituting a relative hegemony where aggression of one against the other is really in-fighting between rulers -- or participant beneficiaries as such. Is 'forcing' a boss to pay a higher wage out of the massive arsenal of state-privilege and blood money to the workers he bosses around and abuses a constitutive act of aggression under libertarian principles? I'm not really sure, but I am open to your view on why it might be. As I've said before, I haven't really made-up my mind on this issue.
