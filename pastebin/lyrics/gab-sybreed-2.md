**June 16th, 2012**

.

~A song on God Is An Automaton revolves around an epistemological issue.

~God Is Automaton will highlight on issues of  the nature of God, the nature of humanity, societal-level issues that every album includes, and the spiritual themes that all of their albums include.

~Ben described himself as misanthropic and described humanity as twisted.

~They hate being compared to Fear Factory. Yes, they literally 'hate' it.

~Type in google: sybreed a radiant daybreak.

~In a recent interview from this March, Ben said that some Australian crowd would be the first to be treated to a full song from the new album live.

~Challenger is going to be on the new album.

~Ben likes the drummer from Gorija.

~In interviews, Ben makes clear that although their music [mainly Slave Design] may sound 'mechanical', that the music is intended to be very much at the level of and about humans. Human emotions, human nature, social structures, etc etc. are key concepts and inspirations. He said that any mechanical feel or reference in their song should be taken to be about society instead, with society being a machine. I have to note that he usually puts things in sophisticated terms and sequences thereof that I have trouble quoting or reproducing in proper form from memory.

~Ben said that he felt that Antares was the biggest progression out of their three albums [this statement was from an interview at the era of Pulse]. Drop said he was impressed with Isolate and Dynamic the most, and Ben said that he was impressed with the chorus in Revive My Wounds.

~Ben is very inspired by Nietzsche: in interviews, he uses a lot phrases and concepts that are 'Nietzschean' in nature. He described The Pulse of Awakening as a very Nietzschean album. He specifically once said that we live in a society where 'god is dead' and 'we want to be god'.

~Ben said that both musically and lyrically what [at least one of the] key concepts of what Sybreed is about is anger. He said that in the past decade, metal is lacking a certain anger and 'madness' [the context heavily implied that he didn't mean 'madness' in any 'being insane' sense, but just intense anger] that he wants Sybreed to bring back to modern metal.

~Ben said that since Slave Design they have become much more interested inand improved with musical theory and songwriting. He said at the beginning that Slave Design was something that was more 'typed out' than it was played. He said in the Slave Design era he had a hard time doing clean vocals, but now that he can do them very easily.

~In general, Ben is very critical of and disapproving of Slave Design across the interviews I have read.

~Ben says in almost every text interview that there is something very wrong with society. He places strong emphasis on how many of much of his lyrical content is inspired by that fact, and he said that the band probably wouldn't exist if it wasn't for that being a fact abou their environment.

~Surprisingly, Ben didn't disagree that generally their music has some kind of futuristic theme, edge, or 'feel' to it. However, he was critical of the interviewer in that he said that being futuristic and criticizing and evaluating modern society and modern issues are not incompatible. He gave a long explanation on how hypothetical future events, concerns, and themes can reflect on modern events in that, for example, we can find ourselves running into apocalypse, or otherwise that any future event the past bears responsibility for, so the present is very relevant to the future. He made clear again what they are about, strongly implying that it is nothing that is so out of touch with today as to be literally interpreted as being relevant only to or taking place in the future.

~Ben mostly approved of the Antares cover art because the back cover highlighted a sort-of 'spacey' feel that he says that the album has, and that the angel at the front cover makes a good highlight of some of the 'apocalyptic/biblical' [exact wording there] themes. He said that he wasn't involved in the making of the cover art, and any reflection on the lyrical themes should be take with the knowledge that it is influenced by the interpretations of the sole person who did the cover art [a name was referenced].

~The core concept of Doomsday Party is that in the end we all die some day. The interviewer for this was an idiot and said that he thought Doomsday Party was an upbeat and 'party type' song. Ben rightfully denounced that description as being false.

~For The Pulse of Awakening, he said each song is focused on some aspect of human misery. He describes the whole of writing them as a process of trying to 'solve' these issues, an endeavour that ultimately fails which is highlighted in 'From Zero To Nothing' [this is not to say that this literally what From Zero To Nothing is about; he just said that he feels the song highlights the 'failure' to solve anything]. He said that in this complete utter failure, that that is when you can experience the 'pulse of awakening'.

~One of the first things I said about Sybreed's lyrics, generally, was how inspired by Nietzsche they seemed to be. Nana-nana-boo-boo!

~In many interviews, 'human emotion' was mentioned as being very centric to their music [unsurprisingly]. Drop said that specifically they love sad emotions and hate happy ones.

~Ben appears to be 'a sophisticate'. When asked if any of their songs are political and what his political views are, he said he 'studied politics' when he was younger but is disillusioned by how 'unrational' people are with it. I think "having political shit fights with peers" would probably be most people's descriptions of their exposure to politics rather than any experience with 'studying politics'.

~Ben has said that the closest thing to a political message Sybreed has come is with Twelve Megatons Gravity, where he said it is critical mainly of 'entertainment society'.

~Ben went on an interesting tangent in one interview about how important it is for individuals to figure out themselves what is moral. This seems to be highlighted in AEON where the religious figurehead says, "I need but despise the very substance of truth 'and define what is right'."

~In several interviews, the concept of 'locating' human misery is brought up. What is hammered most by Ben is that first and foremost thatwhat is wrong in society is what Sybreed is about. However, 'human misery' is constantly mentioned, as well as the concept of 'human emotion', and, specifically in an Antares era interview, how important it was for the correlating [to the lyrics] emotions to be able to be felt in the song.

~I should point out here how a lot of these quotes are consistent with each other across the Antares and Pulse eras, including the concept of understanding and 'locating' human misery, which really makes me interested in the chorus of No Wisdom Brings Solace: "I start to realize the limitations of my attempt to truly understand the misery of man."

~They noted that first and foremost, Sybreed is and always will be a 'heavy' band, whether it is technical or melodic. They said if they ever lost interest in that sound, that they will disband Sybreed to preserve their integrity. Ben said that they hope that this will never happen.

~They made clear their desire to make as many (good) albums as possible. They specifically said that they don't just want to make albums, they want to go ahead and make a lot of them.

~Ben made clear that their progression in musical style and songwriting is something that is intended to continue throughout each successful album.

~Both Drop and Ben described themselves as 'black metalheads'. On that note, Ben said something interesting which was that he embodies all black metal humor, except that there is no jokes contained within it. I think this was supposed to mean that the themes of the songs are intentionally presented in overblown style, but regardless the core of it is very serious and something he is very concerned with.

.

~And now for something completely different...

~I watched a 15 minute interview with The Interbeing, which sounded like an interrogation; the question asker had a voice filter that made him sound like Optimus Prime, and the questions were very assertive and 'authoritive' in nature.

~The singer said that there is an underlying concept to the album, which is that you are following a being based upon the name of the band [The Interbeing] which goes through different roles of dystopian "and also" futuristic inspirations. You follow these being through these roads, which endgs at 'the edge of the obscure'. I think he should have said ledge of oblivion instead. Both are probably acceptable descriptions to him though.

~They are VERY confident in their music. One of the main reasons the EPs was in the main album was because 'they were very good songs'. That is a paraphrase, but he did emphasize that they were not just good but something along the lines of very good.

~This being from just prior to August 2011 - the release of the album - that their plan is to promote the album through touring and marketing, and then make the second album. He [the singer] said that they have already wroten some new material and that 'it sounds kickass'.

~The album took forever to come out because the process of getting a contract with a label and etc. took them forever. They said the album was done at the beginning of 2010.

~They describe their music as a mixture of Industrial Metal, Djent Metal, and Melodic Death Metal 'with a Swedish sound'. They made clear that they like metal and electronic music primarily, and that is what The Interbeing is about musically: the combination of the two.

~Apparently, the writing of the lyrics come much later into the whole song writing process.

~They use 7 string guitar and are considering some use of 8 string for the next album.
