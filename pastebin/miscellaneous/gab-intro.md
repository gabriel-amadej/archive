Hey, I'm Gabriel Amadej. I am a neo-Proudhonian market anarchist interested in the indexical relationship between agorism (or counter-economic practice more precisely) and syndicalism. I feel that both philosophies demand direct action in their own way, and if it isn't necessarily true that counter-economics depends on syndicalistic action and vice versa, then they at least benefit from one another and certainly do not stand in contradiction with each other. I take from Proudhon his precursors to agoristic and syndicalistic theory, his rich sociology, as well as his understanding of political economy as being necessarily grounded in an expressive metaphysics.

For quite some time, I've been interested in developing a wholly secular, 'non-naturalist' metaphysics that rejects both supernaturalism and naturalism along the same lines as its sister category in meta-ethics. Holding like Aristotle did that metaphysics is very much "the first philosophy", this helps form the groundwork with which I attempt to construct and organize my worldview. As a quick example of this, I am interested in adopting Swanton's pluralist virtue-ethics [http://www.mediafire.com/.../%5BChristine_Swanton%5D...] within a libertarian paradigm (e.g. excising appropriate responses to authority as a virtue into a necessary vice) in a way grounded on an entirely platonic understanding of moral properties. This along with my anti-skeptical epistemology gives me a free use of 'tools' to understand and the analyse the world around me in a way that is fairly holistic: that my metaphysics, epistemology, ethics, and politics all relate to each other and mutually support each other in a coherent set of beliefs. My libertarian ethical commitments combined with my fiery moral realism makes my call for anarchism normativity-ladden through and through -- as it damn well should be. The various schools of economic thought, from which I inherit many ideas and owe my allegiance to none, are understood by me in a more-or-less idiosyncratic way as I believe the over-naturalization of the discipline has brought nothing but ill and confusion. If I had to specify my economic influences, I'd say that I border on some kind of synthesis between classicism and Austrianism.

The "neo-Proudhonian" concept comes from Shawn P. Wilbur in his attempt to (a) follow Proudhon's commitment to forever evolving and revising his conceptual projects, such that (b) we can apply this principle of progress to a total re-evaluation of our anarchism. That means just as much discovery of our forgotten traditions as it does constructing new ones. One angle I find inspiring for this project comes from Phillip Pettit's article "Freedom as Antipower", which proposes a conception of freedom as something similar to the civic republican definition as non-domination, but is evidently more developed and soundly spelled out. Pettit even heavily implies the antistatist turn his concept might imply, even though he "doesn't recommend it", and indirectly provides us with a powerful resource for understanding anarchism, what it means to have it, and dissolves the horrendous negative liberty/positive liberty distinction. I won't make this introduction even longer by summarizing "antipower", but you can can find his article here: https://www.princeton.edu/.../FreedomasAntipower_Ethics...

Aside from all that, I am interested in decentralist LGBT+ activism, feminism, and forms of radical labour activism not mentioned; how all it ties into libertarianism and is implied by the same principles that get us to value concepts such as non-invasion, 'freedom', and the autonomy of the individual.

On the subject of quirks and such.

Like Gleason, I am a fan of anime, though a very casual one at that. (I've liked first season Death Note, Ergo Proxy, Kill la Kill, Aku no Hana, Fullmetal Alchemist, Darker Than Black, and Attack on Titan.) I am a literal intellectual hermit that associates with people mostly online, and my formal education is limited to having passed grade seven. I think institutionalized education is a disease, or at least I hold contempt for how it currently stands: bureaucratized, hierarchical, and largely disabled from performing its intended functions. And although I read heavily, I haven't actually read many fictional works. The only thing that really sticks out in my mind is the A Song of Ice and Fire series. Highly recommended.

People that have influenced my political and philosophical developments include Laurence BonJour, Benjamin Tucker/Kevin Carson (same person, really), Pierre-Joseph Proudhon, Voltairine de Cleyre, Michel Foucault, Manuel DeLanda, E.J. Lowe, Shawn P. Wilbur, Cody Wilson, Slavoj Žižek (yes, really), and Michael Huemer.
