There are two main components to llegalist approaches: 

(a) Helping people produce and exchange goods and services - whether its market, barter, or gift economy oriented (or a pluralism of all)- without facing disruptive inspection, time-delayed authorization, costly licensing requirements, selective regulations of business models that automatically privilege a few to dominate economically, zoning codes that define huge populations of people out of self-employment simply housing circumstance, etc etc. by any self-styled authority; 

(b) Counter-economic institutional building. this ranges from various things:

One is showing communities without prior access to capital from the outside economy the means to denominate the value of goods and services between each them. One example would be independent credit clearing system, like that of Tom Geco, which essentially acted as a chequing account  /w permanent overdraft protection -- producing or providing a service nets you a positive balance and you can consume the product/services of others up to a negative balance with some kind of limit that works best on average for them  as decided by the people involved and not some fatcat in city hall.

Another would be competing institutions of the provision of law and justice which, in the traditional 20th mode of organization, seemed unrealistic or doomed to destruction, can now have some grounds to flourish due to open-source projects constantly develop ways that allow something to be open, anonymous, and secure all at the same time. A physical application of the basic mutli-signature trust system used in protocols like Bitcoin -- no faith between any of the parties is assumed, but these exchanges can be dissected, recorded, etc. from the public at large without revealing the identities of actors involved. One of the more obvious applications of this solution would be to other illegal markets, helping to mitigate disputes and violence that emergent not just from a government monopolizing law and excluding it for others, but from there also being a need to secure themselves from the state.

There are a million things that could be listed, but obvious candidates could include a more  radical model of the 2011 Occupy movement we've seen -- i.e. a legitimate attempt to right the wrongs of the theft of the commons beginning from the early modern area. Mass squats and international community building are ways to provide people cheap, sometimes free housing much like what would have been reasonable if it wasn't for the fact that most land currently 'owned' was never even at least homesteaded is either a direct result of (as seen in the case of eminent domain) present policies or the bloody history of  massive expropriation of landed peasants and colonial pre-emption of land for enriched speculators.
