-The properties of being maximally great entail that God is uncaused and eternal because being omnipotent means he has access to all states of time—thus he stretches infinitely into the past, such that no event or object could precede his presence. He would also be immaterial since being a physical would be a limitation of his omnipotence and thus contradict his properties. So this means he doesn't require other things to exist in order for him to exist, and he cannot be destroyed.

-If this we accept that this being is logically possible, then the reason we have to accept that this being is logically necessary (e.g. exists in all logically possible worlds) is because there no conceivable possible world that has conditions such that his existence is negated (which is because he does not require some other contingent entity to cause his existence and he is undestroyable). So any genuinely possible world—i.e. a description of the way the world could be—would have to include God in its conception.
