**Notes on Epistemic Justificaiton from Bonjour**

#### CH. 1-4.

~What confers a held belief epistemic justification is when the subject has an internally accessible reason for thinking
that it is likely to be true.

*Not based on what is actually or externally likely to be true.

~A foundationalism that admits basic sensory beliefs will obviously not be subject to the threat of skepticism.

-However, the traditional conception, that is, foundational beliefs on the basis of some internal mental state, is the one
that is fundamentally correct- and so addressing skepticism is urgent for our purposes.

~An objection to this version of foundationalism is that of asking how the allegedly basic beliefs are themselves justified.

-An answer that says that this issue does not arise for these basic beliefs is obviously an insufficient view.

-An answer that says such beliefs are self-evident or intrinsically justifying is, on the latter account, entirely circular-
which undercuts one of the major objections to coherentism; secondly, it does not seem to allow for defeasible justification-
which is quite a dogmatic and obviously insufficient view.

*The previous answer also cannot satisfy contingent beliefs, those true in some possible worlds and not others, for it cannot be lended to be true simply on the basis of its (contingent) content.

~Some foundationalists (e.g. C. I. Lewis, Richard Fumerton) say that some beliefs are foundationally justified in virtue of our direct acquaintance with the relevant experiential content.

-Is this still an assertive cognitive act, is what is directly apprehended 'given'?

*If yes, then this act of cognition, if itself is justified, can provide a reason for thinking that the belief in quesiton is true.

-The content of this internal apprehension is contingent, so there is still an obvious case for needing to justify it.

*If no, then this state does not have to be justified- but now it's unclear just how the state itself can justify anything.

~A way out of this dilemma is to say that this internal apprehension is semi-assertive in character, and not in a way that raises an additional need for justification.

-Some philosophers have tried to say that this is a rudimentary cognitive state prior to language and conceptualization, it would depict something, yes, but that representative content would be nothing like a propositional assertion.

*This represensational has to have informational content about that, with respect to context a certain belief, that the relevant state of affaris obtains instead of not-obtaining, or being false.

-It is seemingly possible to ask whether this information is correct or incorrect in what it depicts. And so the issue of epistemic justfication raises itself once again.

.

#### CH. 1-5.

~One conception of knowledge is to say that the degree of justification, that is, the internally accessible probability that the belief is likely to be true is something greater than 0.51 out of 1. This is the *weak* conception of knowledge.

-How might the crucial justification here be determined or specified?

-If the levels of justification specie is the traditional one, that is, something like degrees of probability in relation to justifying evidence or premises, then the idea of definative level of justification, short of truth, being sufficient for knowledge generates a serious problem:
*While someone who knows both proposition *A* and *B* would be thought to be able on that basis to know the cojunction proposition A *and* B, the possession by both A and B of any proposed level of probability less than 1 in relation to justifying evidence does not garauntee that their conjunction will possess at least that level of probability, so it's hard to see how the weak conception of knowledge could work- even if a definite level of justification was specified.

.

#### CH. 2.

~It seems that an internalist account of epistemic justification was entirely taken for granted until recent times.

~For the reliabilist ,a belief is justified if it is caused in a way or by a process that makes it objectively likely that the belief in question is true.

-If Amanda had perfect clairvoyence but empirical evidence that it was unreliable; given A, B choices that cater to the two exclusives; it would be justified under a certain type of reliabilism for her to have ignored the evidence and relied on her clairvoyence.

~Qualified externalism holds that a belief is justified if it satisfies a reliable external causal process *and* if the subject possesses no strong evidence, or strong reason of the internalist sort, for doubting the belief.

-But to refute, we just have to modify the Amanda example by making it so that Bertha does not believe that she is clairvoyent.'

~There doesn't seem to be any tenable route for externalism other than being a halfway house.

-Is there any intelligent rationale for, in qualified externalism, for using the *negative* internalist conception of justfication and ignoring the *positive* one?

-A clear reason, e.g. accepting beliefs that are internally unlikely to be true is obviously irrational from an epistemic standpoint that aims at truth.

*But this would support the positive conception too.

.

#### CH. 2-4.

~Here, we consider two primary objections to internalism:

*1. That intellectually and educationally unsophisticated subjects are ascribed very little knowledge under standard internalist views.

*2. All internalist views of justification end up in an extreme skepticism where few, if any, sophisticated adults are justified in their beliefs or to constitute knowledge.

.

#### CH 2-5.

~Internalism and externalism may not be mutually exclusive.

-If we say they are different theories on what constitutes knowledge, then it's to object that there seem to be two spheres of possible knowledge that predominantly exteralist and predominantly internalist in character.

-Perhaps, then, the internalist/externalist debate is best construed in terms of epistemic justification.

*Epistemic justification is usually specified as a type of species of justification that is required for knowledge.

.

#### CH. 2-6.

~Epistemic justification is fundamentally about whether we have any good reasons to think that any of our beiefs about the world are true.

~An internalist conception of justification is drives this purpose in a much more fundamental than way the externalist view because the internalism focuses on what is cognitively accessible and first-person.

*For the externalist, the justification of their belief is based on an external fact for which he has no first-person access. So the conclusion he can draw is that his beliefs in question are reliabily arrived at *if* they are reliable in a way that he believes them to be.

~Commonsense does not commit us to "knowledge" in some unspecified sense, nor that our empirical beliefs are justifed in a sense that is loose enough to admit externalism. Rather, it is the conviction that we have good reasons within our cognitive grasp for thinking that our various beliefs about the world are true.

-Externalism does not even have the ability to make philosophical sense of commonsense, since justification is not inherently available to the subject.

*So it seems that externalism is quite to open to skepticism, since it commits no claims on our internally accessible reasons with which to deal with the more first-person threat of skepticism. And if it is the true solution to the regress problem, then no such perspective is available to anyone.

**Might be able to modify: speak to the possibility that beliefs are reliably caused and the possibility that they are, in an externalist sense, justified- this all would destroy its appeal as a response to skepticism.

.

#### CH. 3.

~Coherentism holds that (1) there are no basic beliefs and (2) primary basis for empirical justification is that such beliefs fit together and support each other in various, detailed ways, thus forming a *coherent* system of beliefs- or perhaps more than one.

-Coherentism seems to be motivated by mere avoidance of foundationalism- it's dialectic- and it has no initial plausibility for itself.

~(1) If epistemic justification depends on coherence of beliefs, then justification requires no contact from world outside of system of beliefs

*How can relation among beliefs involving no contact with world yield reason for thinking that beliefs in question describe that world?

~(2) In principle, it is possible to make an indefinitely many alternative and conflicting coherent belief systems in an arbitrary way, surely they are not all justified in the epistemic sense- of possessing a good reason for thinking their components are likely true.

~(3) Ultimately, there is no clear reason to think coherence of system makes likely that component beliefs are true.

.

#### CH. 3-1.

~Only hope to respond to circularity objection is that proponents assume beliefs are justified in an assymetrical, linear way- but really, they might be nonlinear and holistic; all standing in relation giving mutual support.

-It would be mistake to construe in way of saying that coherentist belief system is an entire body or set of beliefs that stand or fall together.

*What coherentism requires is prime units of justification being interlated beliefs rather than individual beliefs, groups large enough to satisfy various aspects of coherence.

~For coherentism, coherence is a matter of how beliefs in a given belief-system may dovetail with each other.

-Which will depend on explantory and logical relations, one that would allow for comparative assessments (of coherence).

*Unsurprisingly, this is extremely difficult for any serious account of epistemic justiication

**Partly, this is because of the complexity of the overall (coherentist belief system) picture; partly because such an account depends on specific accounts of a larger number of narrower topics, such as induction.

~Any prima facie plausible conception of coherence must go beyond logical consistency amongst the beliefs in a system.

-Coherence requires a high degree of inferential interconnectedness between the beliefs and the system; probalistic, logical, relations of necessity, etc.

-Some philosophers empathize explanatory relations as the criterion of coherence, but is this sufficient?

*Surely, the coherence of a system is enhanced by such, but not all inferential connections can be described by explanatory relations.

-Ultimately, giving a full account of coherence is a largely unsolved project.

~One objection to coherentism is to say that it is obvious that sensory observation plays a central role in empirical justification.

-While this is true causally, this doesn't mean that it necessarily carries a justificatory role.

-So, then, it is possibly the case that, given the previous objection, a coherentist view justifies non-inferential observation still depends on the coherence of background beliefs.

*However, it is crucial that such a justification still depend, in some way, on just how the belief was caused- otherwise we could invent beliefs arbitrarily to fit the coherentist structure.

-Bonjour's own previous approach was by appealing to spontaneous beliefs: one that strikes in the observer in a coercive way.

*The suggestion was, of course, that ordinary observational beliefs as a causal result of sense experience have this sort of character.

**That a belief is spontaneous has no initial bearing, from a coherentist standpoint, on whether the belief is justified; the category would include hunches and the like otherwise.

***The further suggestion, then, is that certain kinds of spontaneous beliefs can be justified within the person's body of beliefs, by appeal to: (i) the fact of their spontaneous occurrence, (ii) the relevant background conditions, (iii) the apparently successful track record with respect to frequency of truth under conditions of spontaneous beliefs of those specific kinds (identified by subject matter, mode of sensory production, etc.)- all of this being assessed within the person's body of beliefs by appeal to coherence.

~First, the other beliefs needed to justify observerational beliefs must themselves be justified in a way that does not collapse into foundationalism.

*Namely: (i) beliefs about the specific conditions under which the spontaneous belief in question occurred, (ii) the belief that cognitively spontaneous beliefs of the specific kind in question are likely, under those specific conditions, to be true, and (iii) further beliefs pertaining to that specific belief occurrence, including the belief, that it was indeed cognitively spontaneous, together with further beliefs that are relevant to specifying the particular belief in question.

**Justification for (i) have to include observational beliefs, themselves justified in some particular fashion, or by a set of mutually supporting observations. The justification for (ii) will appeal inductively to other cases of correct observations, as well to more theoretical reasons for thinking that beliefs of the kind are generally produced in a reliable way. Justfication for (iii) will appeal to introspective beliefs, themselves constituting a species of observation, and ultimately the believer's grasp of relevant parts of his body of beliefs.

-Second, the mere possibility of coherentist observation is alone insufficient to appreciably accomodate the role observation plays in our cognitive lives.

~if by appeal to coherence of the believer's system of beliefs that all issues of epistemic justification is decided, then an internalist coherence theory requires that the believer have adequate and justified grasp of system of beliefs. It is then unclear how this meta-apprehension or meta-belief about the contents of the believer's system is to be justified.

-These metabeliefs are obviously contingent and probably empirical in character, so they must themselves be justified by appeal to the believer's internal system. Yet this seems entirely circular and question-begging.

-Bonjour's own previous approach was to migitate this objection by treating the meta-belief concercing the contents of the belief system as an unjustified hypothesis in relation to which issues of justification are conditionally assessed.

.

#### CH 3-2.

~What seems left is for the coherentist to give argument, from empirical premise saying that belief system is coherent, to conclusion that component beliefs of system is likely, to appropriate degree, to be true. Such argument would have to be a priori in character, any further empirical ingredient would on a coherentist view be justified by appeal to coherence.

-Elsewhere, Bonjour has previously attempted such a meta-justificatory argument by saying that only approximate truth can explain fact of long-run coherence.

.

#### CH 3-3.

~First, it is implausible to suppose that the form of skeptiicsm implied in Bonjour's previous solution is something that just has to be believed with, and, moreover, there may not be a solution compatible with any solution of the problem of accessing one's own beliefs that is compatible with coherentism.

-If Bonjour's previous doxastic presumption were true, it would be a 'reason' that exists in the abstract, but it wouldn't be accessible to the believer.

~Second. The coherentist attempt to make existence of input requirement for justification does not succeed, so long as the requirement is construed as one that is internalistically recognizeable. The reason is that the alternative coherent systems objection arises again: as long as specified within the person's body of beliefs that cognitively  spontaneous beliefs occur, there will be indefinitely many other competing systems containing analogous specifications but characterizing the world in an arbitrarily chosen or delivered way.

-Bonjour's previous attempt to answer this objection was to say that in order to be genuinely justified, such a body of beliefs must actually believed by someone- an arbitrarily chosen system will not remain coherent when in actual use, and these arbitrary systems will not be found to occur in a cognitively spontaneous way.

*This appeals to the direct awareness of one's actual beliefs and their occurrence, which is not legitimately available to the coherentist. As long as the occurrence of spontaneously generated beliefs is assessed only by appeal to coherence of body-belief-system, the objection stands.

~Third. Since a coherentist view must appeal to a sustained coherence of the system, then this raises the issue of how memory beliefs are to be justified. Any initial appeal seems to be viciously circular.

.

#### CH. 4-1.

~The natural way to answer the dilemma noted in CH 1. is to appeal to the conscious experience involved in having the first-order belief or thought in question. This has to be presented in the right way.

-One sort of experience is apperceptive: I may be aware of specific belief by virtue of having higher-order state of awareness that has first-order belief as its object, but is metaphysically distinct from it. However, the content of this higher-order state is the same as the second-order meta-belief, so appealing to it is circular.

-Instead, the essential and intrinsic aspect of having any occurrent belief just *is* being consciously aware of the correlative aspects of its content: first, its propositional content, second, the assertory, e.g. rather than doubting its content. These two awarenesses are not reflective in character. They are partially constitutive of the first-level state of occurent belief or thought itself.

*Thus it is successfully semi-assertive; it is not a second-order apperceptive awareness with propositional content nor a purely non-cognitive awareness of the belief content. It is an intrinsic, constitutive awareness of the specific propositional content of a belief: an awareness that is, partially, an awareness of a certain propositional content, but is not an assertive, higher-order awareness *that* such a proposition is believed.

-For it's non-apperceptive, constitutent character, the issue of justification for is neither required nor indicated.

-And indeed, this intrinsic awareness should be considered infallible. For there is no other element or further, external fact that it could be mistaken about.

*Given this result, the idea is that intrinsic awareness is the meta-justification-immune justifier that the second-level meta-belief may be justified.

~Doesn't one have to still judge that this built-in awareness and that the claimed second-order belief are the same, and doesn't this higher-order judgement itself require some independent justification, thus continuing the regress?

-This type of meta-justificatory demand is not as clear or 'fair', and it is an objectionable case of over-intellectualization.

.

#### CH 4-2.

~Alternative to the previous view of intrinsic mental constituent is "higher-order thought" theory of consciousness.

-In this view, consciousness is not an intrinsic property of any mental state. One mental state becomes conscious only by being the object of a second mental state.
*If true, this would defeat the semi-assertive solution for foundationalim. For reasons already stated, the appeal to the second-order awareness, which seems to be the occurence of the second-order metabelief, is what would be required and would be circular.
