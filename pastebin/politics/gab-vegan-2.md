because i can reasonably see why, for example, the fact that something that is capable of experiencing or otherwise affected by the impact of your actions upon on it is a reason to give it consideration when you do something to it. i don't see why its biological makeup is morally relevant at all; what relations here even connect to it a moral property that is about it?also, i'd even say that "humans" (and other sufficiently similar beings) have certain rights that non-human animals don't, in virtue of things like their capacity for sapience, but some (or most) rights that we confer to humans seem to me only grounded in the relevant aspects of their capacity for sentience. for example, the fact that something is capable of interests seems to me a reason to certain things relevant to interests in considerations. another example is that their right to not be tortured seems to be heavily centered on, or is only relevant to, the fact that they can actually feel it/experience it and be negatively 'affected'--again, in the sense that there is a subject that you can loosely 'go against'-- personally by the action for the first place; it's the reason why you can't 'torture' meaningfully a rock for example. certain mental properties seem to me to be connected to moral properties in an intricate way, whereas mere biological properties seem to have no relation whatsoever.
