Madej:	So how do you appreciably mitigate the externalities of state law and of 'political failures'? Let's say a large enough group of people get together through the democratic process, which I assume your minarchist society would inherit, and repeal a harmful tax policy. Now even the people who didn't even participate in these political efforts has reaped the benefits. Yet if enough people act on their individualized incentives to ride the external benefits and bear none of the costs, then otherwise beneficial changes won't even be facilitated in the first place.

Not only that, but if a group itself provides a public good voluntarily and through market means, the resources they have to expend will pay for the public good. If a group provides itself with a public good through the means of a state, the resources they pay will only extend to covering the overhead of influencing government policy. The government will then finance that public good with taxation. They are unlikely to even bear that much of the costs of pressing for a government-provided public good, or otherwise they would go ahead and do it themselves. And what happens if the delivered public good is not even one of universal benefit to the society? The costs of providing it are widely distributed anyway.
